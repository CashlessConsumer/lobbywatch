# Two papers, one direction: SEBI loosens foreign disclosures, tightens exchange boards

SEBI dropped both of this week's papers on the same day, and they pull in opposite directions.

The headline is the [proposal to remove the Additional Disclosure Framework for FPIs](https://www.sebi.gov.in/reports-and-statistics/reports/nov-2026/consultation-paper-on-proposal-to-review-the-additional-disclosure-framework-for-foreign-portfolio-investors-_105926.html) (20 November, comments due **11 December**). One question — do you agree with removing it? Since August 2023 the framework has forced funds with concentrated or very large India books to disclose every owner down to the last natural person, with no threshold, including subscribers of offshore derivative instruments. SEBI's paper cites ₹5.61 lakh crore ($62.24 billion) of FPI outflows between October 2024 and October 2026 and calls the framework the main deterrent for FPI flows ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/business/markets/sebi-mulls-review-of-additional-disclosure-norms-for-fpis-floats-consultation-paper/article71724977.ece)). The enabling change came from the government: Press Note 2 of 2026 (15 March) redefined "beneficial owner" under Press Note 3 to the money-laundering-rule thresholds, and SEBI's proposal builds on it. The opposition is already on record, unusually early: Congress general secretary Jairam Ramesh called the repeal a threat to market integrity and demanded it be dropped ([Elets BFSI](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/congress-opposes-sebi-plan-to-drop-fpi-disclosure-rules-as-threat-to)). Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey announced the paper hours before it was published ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/sebi-for-removal-of-granular-fpi-fund-ownership-disclosure/articleshow/136043114.cms)). The transparency test is now SEBI's own comment portal — who argues what on a disclosure regime, published where all can read it.

The counterpoint landed the same day: a [second consultation](https://www.sebi.gov.in/reports-and-statistics/reports/nov-2026/consultation-paper-on-strengthening-the-governance-of-market-infrastructure-institutions-miis-_105928.html) would cap a market infrastructure institution MD at 12 years across all MIIs, cut the public interest director age limit from 75 to 70 with a two-term cap, and seat AMFI's CEO on the AMC Repo Clearing Corporation board ([CorpLawUpdates](https://www.corplawupdates.in/updates/sebi-consultation-paper-mii-governance-tenure-caps)) — the third paper in a chain that began in June 2025. Looser ownership disclosure abroad, tighter tenure rules at home; the interests touched by each will read both.

At IFSCA, a [working-group report on insurance-linked securities](https://www.corplawupdates.in/updates/ifsca-insurance-linked-securities-framework-gift-ifsc) (submitted 19 October, listed 16 November) recommends catastrophe-bond vehicles exempt from the Insurance Act's minimum capital and solvency requirements, pending a statutory amendment — and it is chaired by T. L. Alamelu, formerly IFSCA's Principal Advisor. A former insider's report proposing carve-outs from the parent statute is a door-adjacent document this register will track.

IRDAI's [subsidiary-instructions exposure draft](https://lobbywatch.cashlessconsumer.in/consultations.html) closes **today** — and its own [exposure-drafts listing](https://irdai.gov.in/exposure-drafts) rendered "temporarily unavailable" on recheck this morning, after omitting the draft on 9 and 16 November. The flag stands; that listing gap is precisely what the [comments-disclosure RTI template](https://lobbywatch.cashlessconsumer.in/access.html) exists for. Countdowns: RBI PSL consolidation due **26 November**, SEBI AML/CFT certification **27 November**, both IFSCA papers **30 November**.
