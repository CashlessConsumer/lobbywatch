An extension, a new approval route, and two transparency tests

From 19–26 October, the consultation ledger records two material changes. On 24 October, IRDAI extended the comment deadline for its insurance-distribution reforms from 25 to 31 October, reporting more than 650 comments received so far. The official release gives a count, not a breakdown of who commented or what positions they took. LobbyWatch still marks publication of comments and a response statement as unknown in its consultation ledger. A large submission count shows activity; it cannot by itself show whose views were represented.

On 22 October, SEBI opened a consultation on an automatic approval route for specified related activities by market infrastructure institutions. The paper proposes that recognised stock exchanges and depositories—but not recognised clearing corporations—could use the route for defined activities without prior SEBI approval. Fund deployment would be capped at the lower of 5% of net worth or the regulatory minimum; each instance would require governing-board approval, with half-yearly reporting to SEBI. Comments are due 12 November. The investor test is whether board controls and periodic reporting provide adequate scrutiny where case-by-case prior approval is removed. This is a proposal, not a rule in force.

SEBI’s issue-proceeds monitoring and SME IPO consultations also remain open, with deadlines on 3 and 4 November respectively.

The doors ledger records no appointment dated 19–26 October. The RTI log still has two template-ready requests and no filed request. These are gaps in LobbyWatch’s records, not proof that no meetings or requests occurred. This week brings process movement—not evidence of who influenced a final rule. The next test is whether the submissions and regulatory reasoning become visible.
