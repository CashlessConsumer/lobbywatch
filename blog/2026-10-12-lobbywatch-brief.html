An empty week is not proof of no access

The public record we track was quiet from 5 to 12 October. The consultation ledger contains no opening or closing dated in that interval, and the doors register contains no appointment dated in it either. This is a finding about the dates recorded in our ledgers—not evidence that no regulator-industry contact took place.

The RTI log has two entries, both marked template-ready: one asks for comments and response statements on named consultations; the other seeks minutes of meetings where industry bodies were heard. Neither entry is a filed request. A template is a prompt to seek a record, not evidence that a request was submitted, answered, rejected or appealed.

That distinction matters. LobbyWatch can document influence only when a dated public source, a published attendee list, minutes, a comment record or an RTI reply leaves a paper trail. The consultation and doors pages therefore describe what has been found and sourced, not a complete census of access. An empty week in these registers is an absence of newly logged evidence, not proof of an absence of meetings or influence.

For consumers, the practical gap is accountability: without public minutes or identifiable consultation feedback, we cannot compare who had a chance to shape a rule with whose costs the final rule imposes. But this week’s ledgers do not establish that any group received privileged access or that any comment changed a rule. Making that claim without records would turn a transparency problem into an insinuation.

Next update: add a movement only when its date and source can be checked; keep unknown where comment publication or access remains undocumented. That is less dramatic than guessing, and more useful to the person paying.
