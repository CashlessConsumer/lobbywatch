Layer 3 · rule-buyers · the sousveillance stack

Who buys the rules of Indian finance?

Regulators write the rules; RegTrac watches the writers. SROs borrow them; SROTrac watches the borrowers. This register watches the buyers: the interests that flood consultations, get the meetings, and hire the regulators after retirement. Access and influence are documented through published comments, RTI replies, minutes and appointment orders — not vibes.

Comment-transparency scorecard

The first test of a captured consultation is whether anyone can see what was said to the rule-writer. Stance per desk, as verified on the regulator's own site.

DeskStatutePublishes comments?Documented practice
Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016 yes Runs a Public Comments section; its discussion papers instruct commenters to submit through the website with stakeholder categories, and comments are published there. source
International Financial Services Centres Authority IFSCA Act 2019 unknown Consultation papers listed on ifsca.gov.in. Whether comments received are published is unverified — flagged for checking. source
Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India IRDA Act 1999 unknown Consultation papers published on its portal. In June 2026 IRDAI proposed a formal regulation-making framework: minimum 21-day public comment window and a general statement of response to comments published by the date regulations are notified. Until notified, publication of comments is not a settled practice. source
Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority PFRDA Act 2013 unknown Consultation papers published on the site (e.g. Sept 2025 valuation-alignment paper via PIB). Whether comments received are published is unverified — flagged for checking. source
Reserve Bank of India Reserve Bank of India Act 1934 no No general practice of publishing comments received on discussion papers. Commenters self-publish instead (NASSCOM posted its own submission on the Charges paper; Vidhi posted its 2019 PA/PG submission). Individual comment-archives exist only because commenters chose to publish. RTI is the fallback route. source
Securities and Exchange Board of India SEBI Act 1992 yes Posts consultation papers and the comments received on its public-comments portal; a board-approved procedure (18 Dec 2024) formalised pre-legislative consultation for regulation-making. source

Why the person paying should care: when comments are secret, you cannot tell whether a rule was rewritten for the industry that asked — or tested against the public that pays. RBI's 2022 Charges-in-Payment-Systems paper drew submissions from every major industry body; none were published, and the commenters had to self-publish. A rule made in a closed room still lands on your bill.
